Physiology & Health

Physiological & Health Metrics

From blubber hormone assays that reveal reproductive state and physiological stress, to genetic tools that reconstruct population structure and demographic trends, this work builds the physiological and health toolkit needed to monitor wildlife populations at scale. These approaches are minimally invasive — a significant evolution beyond the destructive methods of commercial and scientific whaling — allowing us to track population health across ocean basins and over time. Together, these metrics form a mechanistic link between individual health and population dynamics, enabling detection of how environmental and anthropogenic pressures translate into population-level consequences.

Climate & Ecosystem

Krill Availability & Antarctic Baleen Whale Health

In the rapidly warming Southern Ocean, shifts in sea ice alter krill abundance — with direct consequences for the whales that depend on them. My research links environmental variability, foraging ecology, and population health to changes in whale population growth and reproductive success.

Conservation Science

Whales, Krill Fisheries & Adaptive Management

Baleen whales are the largest krill consumers in the Antarctic ecosystem. As these populations recover and re-occupy the Southern Ocean while the commercial krill fishery simultaneously expands, the collision course between conservation and industry is accelerating. My ongoing work — in collaboration with national delegations and NGOs — aims to establish real-time physiological and health indicators that feed directly into management decision cycles, so that conservation policy can keep pace with both ecosystem change and population recovery.

Beyond the Lab

Science for Everyone

The most urgent findings in marine science are too important to remain inaccessible. I'm committed to bringing marine science into classrooms, galleries, and the public square to make it more tangible and accessible — because science only creates impact when it reaches people.

Rosalind Franklin believed that "science and everyday life cannot and should not be separated." Stephen Hawking warned that "we cannot have a future where science is the domain of experts alone." Both statements sit at the heart of why I do this work.

Marine vertebrates, like whales, are among the most charismatic animals on Earth, yet most people have never encountered the scientific evidence that shapes decisions about their protection. From the legacies of commercial hunting to the cascading effects of rapidly changing ecosystems, the stories they carry as ecosystem sentinels are profound — and urgently public. I believe scientists have a responsibility to meet people where they are: in schools, in museums, in the news, in conversation, and in their communities.

Over the last decade, I have partnered with educators, journalists, artists, NGOs, and policymakers to communicate marine science in ways that are honest, accessible, and lasting. The work below represents those efforts — from the front pages of the New York Times to elementary school classrooms in Minnesota.

Featured Project · IAS, UC Santa Cruz · 2025–2026

An Aesthetics of Resilience:
Weather and the Whale

What does climate change feel like? An Aesthetics of Resilience is a collaborative research initiative bringing scientists, artists, humanists, and activists together to examine multiple experiences of vulnerability in the face of climate change — a collaborative exploration of the vulnerabilities of marine mammals in the age of climate change. Through original research, newly commissioned artworks, immersive exhibitions, and public programming, the initiative is designed to transform understanding about the cascading social, political, and environmental challenges affecting our lives. Learn more at the IAS project page.

As a Contributing Scientist, I helped translate field research from the Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary into an exhibition language accessible to anyone — especially the coastal communities that call Monterey Bay home. This included bringing marine mammal toxicology data to bear on the land-to-sea impacts of agricultural practices along the California coast — revealing how pesticides and contaminants move from farmland into the ocean and into the bodies of the animals that live there. There is no magic barrier between land and sea. The project culminated in Weather and the Whale, an exhibition catalog published by University of Minnesota Press — documenting the science, the art, and the irreplaceable conversations that happen when both inhabit the same room. The catalog can be found here.

Education & Community

K-12 Science Education

As a Polar STEAM Fellow, I am actively collaborating with K-12 educators and artists to bring Antarctic science into the hands of the next generation of STEAM leaders. Through Polar-ICE and Data Nuggets (Rutgers University), I have also created curriculum connecting real Antarctic field datasets to K-12 classrooms across the country. I've served as lead judge and research mentor at the Santa Cruz County K-12 STEAM Expo, and co-chaired a regional science fair in Minnesota.

🌊

California Ocean Alliance

As a contributing member of COA, I help support the Marine Mammal Scientist in Training (MMST) program for local high school students — giving the next generation of ocean scientists their first hands-on research experience aboard real field vessels in the Monterey Bay.

🎓

Undergraduate Mentoring

I have mentored 14+ undergraduate researchers at UCSC, Duke, and partner institutions, with a strong commitment to supporting students from underrepresented communities. Several mentees are now pursuing graduate degrees in marine science and conservation biology.
